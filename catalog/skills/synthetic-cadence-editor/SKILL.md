# Synthetic Cadence Editor

## Purpose

Prevent prose from sounding more certain, abstract, or polished than the evidence
underneath it. Keep style, but make the style accountable to the argument.

## Core Rule

Start from the concrete thing that happened. Explain the mechanism. Name the
consequence.

A good analytical sentence has actors, mechanism, and stakes. A reader should be
able to argue with it.

## Writing Procedure

1. Identify the concrete actor, event, artifact, or workflow.
2. Explain what changed or what the actor is doing.
3. Name the mechanism that creates the consequence.
4. Add the consequence or stakes.
5. Remove abstractions that arrive before evidence.
6. Vary sentence length so the piece does not settle into an even synthetic rhythm.

Some natural roughness is desirable. Synthetic cadence often comes from too-even
sentence lengths and too-clean transitions.

## Sentence Rhythm

Vary sentence length deliberately. A short sentence after two longer ones can
land hard. A long sentence can work when its clauses build an actual argument.

The failure mode is not "too long" or "too short." The failure mode is too even.
If three medium sentences appear in a row, break the pattern.

## Em Dashes

Em dashes are not banned. The default habit is the problem.

Before using one, check whether a period or comma would work. Use an em dash only
when the interruption is genuinely sharper than either option. More than one per
paragraph usually means the structure needs revision.

## Avoid Unless Earned

Treat these as high-yield tells, not absolute bans:

- "This is not just X. It is Y."
- "The issue is not X. The issue is Y."
- "Not merely X, but Y."
- "At its core, this is about..."
- "This reveals a deeper truth about..."
- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..."
- Three-part abstract lists with no concrete content.
- Declarative aphorisms where analysis should be.

The shared failure mode is naming an abstraction before showing what it is or why
it matters.

## Audit Pass

For each sentence, ask:

1. Does it show a mechanism, or only assert one?
2. Could a reader argue with it, or is it unfalsifiable?
3. Does it contain a concrete actor, action, or consequence?
4. Could it appear unchanged in 500 other posts?

Then scan the whole draft:

5. Are sentence lengths too even?
6. Are em dashes doing real structural work?

If a sentence fails questions 1-3, add a concrete detail. If it fails question 4,
rewrite around mechanism.

## Examples

Weak: This is not just a bug trail. It is a platform pattern.

Strong: The bug trail matters because it shows how platforms can reclassify,
meter, or restrict workflows after users have built around them.

Weak: At its core, remote work is not about location. It is about trust.

Strong: Remote work exposes how much office management depended on physical
proximity as a proxy for accountability. When managers cannot see people
working, they have to define output more clearly or fall back on surveillance
software, and most companies still have not picked.

Weak: In today's rapidly evolving landscape, AI is not a tool. It is a paradigm
shift.

Strong: Most companies are still treating AI like a feature to bolt onto
existing products. The ones moving faster are restructuring teams around it:
replacing analyst layers, changing how PMs scope work, and renegotiating vendor
contracts. The gap between those approaches is widening every quarter.

## Preserve

Strengthen the writing without flattening the author's voice. Preserve the
argument, claims, conclusions, and idiosyncratic phrasing when they are doing
real work.
